The inference, in my own words: this is the chain I hold in my head when food inflation moves — it's rarely the headline itself that matters, it's the second- and third-order cost it pushes through fertiliser and transport, which eventually shows up as firmer transport-fuel demand. Where I'd flag it as an opportunity: Nigerian infrastructure investment tends to run the same chain in reverse, signalling appetite for oil to support growth. Where I'd flag it as a threat: a fast food-inflation move with no matching wage growth risks demand destruction further down the chain, which would show up first in Gasoline, not Crude.
